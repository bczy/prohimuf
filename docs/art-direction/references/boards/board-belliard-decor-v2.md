## Validation (Bertrand, 2026-07-18)

- **Axis 1 — tags: KEEP, with a vertical-taper nuance.** Dense tags at street level
  (ground floor + roll-down shutters), but **rarefying fast with height**: much rarer
  at human height, **almost nonexistent at the top**; the upper facade stays clean.
  Explicit caution — _do not overload_; density is a street-level band, not a
  full-facade wash.
- **Axis 2 — spacing: KEEP as corrected.** Tighter than the current isolated look but
  NOT jointif: fine sky slivers between most facades, one slightly wider gap read as a
  narrow passage / bare mur pignon.
- **Axis 3 — trottoirs: KEEP.** Rebuild the cross-section with a trottoir on both
  sides (near-side player band + far-side band at the foot of the buildings), granite
  kerbs, chaussée between; far-side trottoir kept narrow so it doesn't read as a
  second lane.

## Context (why this v2 exists)

7 transparent building-facade PNGs (distinct Paris faubourg immeubles, painterly
cartoon-realistic B&W-leaning night look) currently sit on a shared continuous road
band. Bertrand flagged three things to fix before regen:

1. Walls read too clean — need much heavier tag/graffiti/sticker/stencil/flyer coverage,
   especially at ground-floor/shutter level.
2. Buildings currently read with visible sky gaps between them that are too wide —
   he wants those gaps narrowed for a tighter faubourg rhythm, but NOT fully closed:
   small breathing gaps, narrow sky slivers, an occasional thin alley or exposed mur
   pignon between tronçons should remain (not immeubles jointifs butted edge-to-edge).
3. The street cross-section is missing a sidewalk on the far side (building side) —
   only a near-side band exists. Needs trottoirs on both sides of the roadway, kerbs
   included, closer to an earlier iteration that had them.

All three stay inside the D1 crade-documentaire register already validated (grain,
contrasty B&W, dirty/real — not the neon-forward layer) and inside the 1998 Paris
setting; nothing here reopens the house style or the scope guard.

## Axis 1 — Murs plus taggués

**Proposed direction:** push ground-floor and shutter zones to the D1
crade-documentaire ceiling — treat them as a competition wall (tags stacked over
tags, throw-ups, stickers, pochoirs, a stapled flyer or two), specifically at
Petite-Ceinture/Stalingrad-era density, not a generic "street art poster" wash. Upper
floors stay comparatively calmer (period signage, shutter paint wear) so the eye still
reads architecture above head height — the density lives where hands could actually
reach.

- [Karim Boukercha, Yves Marchand & Romain Meffre — _Graffiti Général_ (Éditions
  Dominique Carré, 2013), publisher page](https://www.editionscarre.com/livres/graffiti-general/) —
  the reference for wall-competition density itself: photo-documents Paris's derelict
  building sites and rail edges (Belleville, la Villette, **Petite Ceinture**,
  Stalingrad) through the late 80s–90s, exactly the register of "saturated, not
  curated" wall coverage this axis asks for.
- [Autour de Paris — "Le terrain de Stalingrad, lieu mythique de l'histoire hip-hop"](https://autour-de-paris.com/project/terrain-stalingrad-lieu-mythique-histoire-hip-hop) —
  the Stalingrad terrain (18e-adjacent, Bd de la Chapelle), Europe's biggest graffiti
  ground at the time before 1990s construction closed it; names the crews (CTK, BBC,
  TCG, NTM, Lokiss) that anchor "who painted this" if a hand-lettering mood is needed.
- [hipopen.net — interview on the Stalingrad terrain](https://hipopen.net/tag/premier-terrain-de-graffiti-stalingrad/) —
  first-person account of the terrain's density and role as a hub, corroborating detail
  for the same spot.
- [Not Only Hip Hop — "La Petite Ceinture : retour sur un graffiti spot parisien"](https://www.notonlyhiphop.com/graffiti/la-petite-ceinture-graffiti-spot-parisien.html) —
  the disused rail line (which Rue Belliard itself runs alongside, per D4) as a
  graffiti corridor since the early 90s; writers named: Trane, Seno, See, PCP, TPK,
  357, Bansa — direct textural link between the street's own geography and the tag
  density this axis wants.
- [drips.fr — "Quand Paris était couvert de tags"](https://www.drips.fr/quand-paris-etait-couvert-de-tags-ou-la-nostalgie-des-90s/) —
  already cited in D1 for shutter-specific texture; re-flagged here as the ceiling
  reference for **how much** is "more taggué" — shutters as a competing-hand-lettering
  surface, not a single clean piece.
- _Risk:_ same cliché-drift flag as D1 — "more tags" must stay anchored to
  ground-floor/shutter competition-wall texture, not gallery-grade single pieces or a
  generic spray-can poster look; and must not silently reintroduce anachronistic
  post-1998 styles (wildstyle pieces from 2000s+ bombing culture look different from
  late-80s/90s hand-tags — keep the letterforms period-correct per Paris Tonkar, D1).
- _Licence:_ _Graffiti Général_ is an in-print, copyrighted book — reference/study
  only, never scan-and-paste (same regime as D1's Paris Tonkar); drips.fr photos are
  third-party-sourced per the site's own credit line, reference only; Autour de
  Paris / hipopen.net / Not Only Hip Hop are editorial blog content, citable as
  historical/context sources, not image sources to lift from directly.

## Axis 2 — Moins d'espace entre les immeubles (tighter, still gapped — not jointif)

**CORRECTED per Bertrand (relayed by orchestrator, post-draft):** this is **not** a
call for a fully continuous mitoyen wall. He wants the gaps reduced from the current
isolated-building look, but small breathing gaps must remain — narrow sky slivers,
an occasional thin alley or exposed mur pignon between two tronçons — a tighter faubourg
rhythm, not buildings butted edge-to-edge.

**Proposed direction:** regenerate the facade row noticeably tighter than the current
pass (narrow the sky gaps substantially), but keep each of the 7 facades a genuinely
separate volume — thin vertical slivers of sky/alley between most of them, and at least
one slightly wider gap read as a real narrow passage or an exposed blank mur pignon
(the "residual wall" left when a neighbour was rebuilt or demolished, common Paris
faubourg accident). This matches Rue Belliard's actual fabric better than either
extreme: it's ordinary 18e streetwall, not a Haussmannian unbroken cornice line, but
also not the wide detached-house gaps currently generated.

- [Wikimedia Commons — Category: Rue Belliard (Paris)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Rue_Belliard_%28Paris%29) —
  18 street-view photographs of the actual street; the direct massing/alignment
  reference to check the regenerated row against — real Belliard buildings sit close
  but not fused, useful for calibrating how narrow "tighter" should read.
- [paris1900.lartnouveau.com — "La rue Belliard"](https://paris1900.lartnouveau.com/paris18/rues/rue_belliard.htm) —
  period-photo history page for the street itself; confirms the ordinary faubourg
  building-to-building rhythm (narrow individual frontages, ordinary party walls)
  predates 1998 by a century — same fabric, just aged, and not a fused wall.
- [Wikipédia FR — Pignon (architecture)](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pignon_%28architecture%29) —
  history of the mur pignon (gable end wall), historically used as a party wall
  between adjoining buildings; direct reference for what an exposed blank gable wall
  in a narrow gap actually looks like architecturally (no windows, raw masonry) if one
  tronçon shows a slightly wider gap.
- [demainlaville.com — "Murs pignons et cours intérieures : des espaces vitaux à
  réinvestir"](https://www.demainlaville.com/murs-pignons-et-cours-interieures-des-espaces-vitaux-a-reinvestir/) —
  describes exactly the "residual" blank gable wall left when a neighbouring building
  is demolished/rebuilt — untreated masonry, sometimes carrying old advertising —
  the direct texture reference for the "occasional thin alley/mur pignon" beat, not a
  generic clean gap.
- [Wikipédia FR — Passage (architecture)](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passage_%28architecture%29) —
  typology of the narrow Paris passage/alley between buildings; grounds the "thin
  alley" option in a real, common Paris urban form rather than an invented gap.
- _Why it serves Belliard:_ keeps the 18e faubourg read (ordinary, imperfect, slightly
  irregular street wall) rather than swinging to either extreme — the wide-gap look
  Bertrand is moving away from, or a fused Haussmannian cornice the street never had.
- _Risk:_ "tighter" is a scale/degree call, not a binary — must be validated in-scene
  (screenshot at play camera) rather than judged on the reference photos alone, since
  the right gap width is a composition question for `lead-art`, not something these
  references settle by themselves; also avoid making every gap read the same width —
  real faubourg rhythm is irregular, not a repeating module.
- _Licence:_ no licence concern (public-street architecture photography / Wikipedia
  history articles); Commons photos and demainlaville.com images are reference/massing
  checks only, not source images to composite.

## Axis 3 — Trottoirs des deux côtés (near + far sidewalk, kerbs)

**Proposed direction:** rebuild the street cross-section as roadway + kerb + trottoir
on BOTH sides — a near-side trottoir band in the foreground (player/action space) and
a far-side trottoir directly at the foot of the building row (buildings sit on a
pavement band, not dropped straight onto the asphalt), each with a granite kerb
(bordure) separating it from the chaussée. Keep the D1 crade-documentaire palette
(worn asphalt, dirty bitumen, no fresh-paved look).

- [Histoires de Paris — "Les chaussées et trottoirs des rues au XIXe siècle"](https://www.histoires-de-paris.fr/chaussees-trottoirs-rues-xixe-siecle/) —
  the base cross-section reference: trottoirs (established citywide by 1846 rules
  under Rambuteau), granite kerbs (bordures, 10–15cm high) separating pavement from
  chaussée, paved/asphalt roadway — the structural template for "sidewalk both sides
  - kerb + road" that this axis needs.
- [L'Association des Professionnels du Pavage (APP) — "Petite histoire du pavage
  parisien"](https://www.assopaveurs.fr/petite-histoire-du-pavage-parisien-2/) —
  materials reference for the chaussée surface itself (grès/granite pavés, standard
  ~15×15cm units) to keep the roadway texture period-correct alongside the trottoirs.
- [Ville de Paris — "Le pavé parisien à l'épreuve du temps"](https://www.paris.fr/pages/le-pave-parisien-a-l-epreuve-du-temps-7511) —
  official municipal page on the pavé parisien, corroborating source for roadway
  material and maintenance look (worn, patched — matches crade-documentaire, not a
  showroom street).
- [Joanne Rasse — "La rue Parisienne" (profil de rue)](https://rassejoanne.com/architecture/levolution-du-profil-de-rue-et-la-place-du-pieton-dans-la-ville/) —
  diagrammatic overview of the Paris street profile (building line → trottoir → kerb →
  chaussée → kerb → trottoir → building line), the clearest single reference for the
  symmetric both-sides cross-section Bertrand is asking to restore.
- _Internal continuity note (not a web reference):_ the current `street` layer
  (`docs/art-direction/prompt-drafts/level-street.md`) is a flat overhead road plane
  world-locked behind the facade, visible only in a narrow QTE band, with **no
  sidewalk at all** in its prompt (bitumen + zebra crossing only) — this axis is
  asking for a structurally different, wider cross-section for the main decor pass,
  not a tweak of that QTE strip; flagging so the two don't get conflated at prompt-
  writing time.
- _Risk:_ widening the cross-section to fit two trottoirs changes the world-space
  footprint of the road band the 7 facades already sit on — a layout/scale question
  for `lead-art`/`dev-r3f-render`, not something this reference set resolves; also
  keep the far-side trottoir narrow enough that it doesn't read as a second roadway
  lane (period trottoirs were narrower than the chaussée until later widenings).
- _Licence:_ no licence concern (historical/technical reference articles, no images
  to lift).
