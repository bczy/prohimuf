# Reference board — Rue Belliard street-facade backdrop (LEVEL DÉCOR family)

Hunt run by `graphic-references` (Ray), relayed by the orchestrator. Family = **level
décor**; this board covers **Rue Belliard only** — one board per family/asset as agreed
with Bertrand.

**Status: VALIDATED by Bertrand (2026-07-18)** — KEEP on all five directions (D1–D5).

## Hunt context (Round 1 — Bertrand's answers)

1. One board per family; décor family goes first, and within it: **Rue Belliard only**.
2. Same inspiration seed as the house style — Paris 1998, clandestine-rave universe.
3. Tonality: **crade-documentaire** — photocopy grain, contrasty B&W, dirty/real. NOT
   the saturated acid-neon register for this décor pass (neon stays the house-style's
   accent layer elsewhere, not the dominant note on this façade).
4. Technique: **everything matters, no single support dominates** — period photos,
   flyers, signage, lettering/stencil are all fair game together.
5. Avoid **anachronisms** above all: no smartphones, no modern CRS riot gear, no
   recent cars/bikes, no contemporary shopfront branding.
6. **No obligation to stay literal to _Prohibition_ (1987)** — free to interpret 1998.
7. Bertrand supplied reference seeds to anchor the hunt (see directions below for the
   verified, stable version of each): "billy la banlieue", a Paris 1990 shopping-street
   photo (Marks & Spencer + RER sign, Alamy 2F60CJ9), a Paris 90s street photo (Flickr),
   a devantures/shutters piece (drips.fr), a Le Parisien archive photo, and a Getty
   "rue de Buci circa 1990" photo.

## Validated directions

### D1 — Crade-documentaire: tagged shutters & the pre-1999 wall

- [Paris Tonkar (Tarek Ben Yakhlef & Sylvain Doriath, Éditions Massot, 1991) — writeup](https://urbaneez.art/magazine/paris-tonkar-the-reissue-of-the-cult-book-on-parisian-graffiti) —
  the founding French graffiti book: B&W photo documentation of Paris walls 1987–1991;
  primary source for hand-lettering density and wall-competition composition.
- [Paris Tonkar — full scan, Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/paris-tonkar-4-ans-de-graffitis) —
  same book, direct page-by-page reference for letterform/fill study.
- [drips.fr — "Quand Paris était couvert de tags, ou la nostalgie des 90's"](https://www.drips.fr/quand-paris-etait-couvert-de-tags-ou-la-nostalgie-des-90s/) —
  photo essay on Paris metal shutters (rideaux de fer) layered in hand-tags through
  the 90s; direct texture reference for the Belliard shopfront shutters.
- Historical anchor (no link needed, dating context only): Mayor Tibéri's 1999
  "Opération Murs propres" (80M francs/yr with Korrigan) ended this look city-wide —
  by our 1998 setting the tagged-shutter aesthetic is still fully alive, which places
  it correctly just before the cleanup.
- _Why it serves Belliard:_ the single most on-point crade-documentaire texture —
  grimy shutters, competing hand lettering, legible even desaturated — matches the
  "everything matters" layering brief directly.
- _Risk:_ cliché drift — "Paris covered in tags" can slide into generic street-art
  poster imagery if not kept anchored to _shutters/storefronts specifically_, not
  gallery-grade pieces.
- _Licence:_ Paris Tonkar is a copyrighted, in-print book (2024 reissue by Massot) —
  reference/study only, never scan-and-paste; drips.fr photos are third-party
  Instagram-sourced per the site's own credit line — reference only, do not lift images
  into an asset.

### D2 — The ordinary 1990s Paris shopfront (anachronism control)

- [Alamy — "Paris shopping street" search (ref 2F60CJ9)](https://www.alamy.com/stock-photo/paris-shopping-street.html?sortBy=relevant) —
  wet winter 1990 Paris shopping street: Marks & Spencer storefront, blue RER station
  sign, period cars, shoppers; the anachronism firewall for signage typography and
  street furniture.
- [Getty Images — "Rue de Buci" photo corpus, circa 1990](https://www.gettyimages.com/photos/rue-de-buci) —
  awning styles, shopfront signage, street furniture of a comparable central-Paris
  street in the right decade.
- Le Parisien photo archive (ref VHA7F5DVUVAUPFW437FTHMNZO4, as supplied by Bertrand) —
  period storefront/street reportage; **link unstable at hunt time, needs
  re-verification before an asset build leans on it directly.**
- _Why it serves Belliard:_ period-correct signage typography, car silhouettes,
  shutter colours, RER/métro signage — the check against anything modern slipping in
  (no roll-up modern branding, no contemporary vehicles).
- _Risk:_ stock-photo licence — Alamy and Getty are commercial libraries. Usable as
  **mood/shape reference only**, never traced or composited directly into a shipped
  asset.
- _Licence:_ commercial stock (Alamy/Getty) — reference/mood only, no redistribution;
  Le Parisien archive photo is press-agency owned, same restriction.

### D3 — Clandestine rave graphic layer (flyers, squat entrances, stencils)

- [Dizonord — "Paris Early Rave Flyers"](https://www.dizonord.fr/product/1708501294/Dizonord-Paris-Early-Rave-Flyers) —
  15,000+ party-flyer graphic-design archive, Paris 1990–95; source for stencil/stamp
  marks and photocopy-flyer graphic language.
- [Printed Matter — "Paris Rave Flyers 1991–1994" (Colpa Press)](https://www.printedmatter.org/catalog/tables/31693/60651) —
  published flyer collection, same period; already banked in
  `docs/art-direction/references/LICENSES.md` §2 under the house-style's own
  rave-flyer/xerox-fanzine reference set.
- [Red Bull Music Academy Daily — "A Brief Republic of Partying"](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2016/04/french-early-rave-feature/) —
  French early-rave feature: squats (Ivry telecom factory), warehouses, scouting
  culture; context for what an entrance marker/stencil is doing on a wall in the
  first place.
- _Why it serves Belliard:_ the layer that turns a generic Paris street into _our_
  street — a hand-stencilled entrance marker, a stapled photocopied flyer on a
  shutter — the "something is happening tonight" tell, kept restrained (neon stays
  the house-style's accent elsewhere, not the dominant note here).
- **Explicit scope guard (redundancy check):** the flyer/fanzine graphic language is
  already the house-style's core reference bank
  (`docs/art-direction.md`, and `docs/art-direction/references/LICENSES.md` §2). This
  direction on the Belliard board is scoped strictly to what is **pasted or painted ON
  the façade itself** (a flyer stapled to a shutter, a stencil sprayed on a wall) — not
  a restatement of the general house-style mood, which is already banked and does not
  need re-curating here.
- _Risk:_ the named redundancy above — if not held to "on the façade only," this
  direction adds nothing new to the décor board.
- _Licence:_ see `docs/art-direction/references/LICENSES.md` §2 — reference pages
  only, do not commit scans; Rave Preservation Project / Phatmedia archives are
  third-party collections, same restriction.

### D4 — Rue Belliard itself: ordinary 18e fabric vs. the one landmark

- [Wikipédia FR — Rue Belliard (Paris)](<https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rue_Belliard_(Paris)>) —
  street created 1868, 18e arrondissement, runs along the old Petite Ceinture rail
  line (south side); grounds the façade in ordinary working artisan/residential 18e
  fabric bordering derelict rail infrastructure, not touristy central Paris.
- [Paris ZigZag — L'immeuble Deneux, 185 rue Belliard](https://www.pariszigzag.fr/insolite/lieux-insolites/immeuble-deneux/) —
  1913 ceramic-tiled modernist façade, the street's one architectural landmark
  (bow-windows, tiled courses); usable as a "single distinctive silhouette" beat in an
  otherwise plain street.
- [PSS-Archi — 185, rue Belliard, building record](https://www.pss-archi.eu/immeubles/FR-75056-2756.html) —
  architectural record for the same building, secondary confirmation source.
- _Why it serves Belliard:_ names what the street actually is instead of a generic
  "Paris street" — plain 18e fabric with one ornate exception, useful as a texture and
  massing reference even when the level doesn't depict this exact block.
- _Risk:_ the Deneux building is a specific, identifiable, heritage-protected real
  building — using its tiled façade risks reading as a direct copy of a named
  building rather than a generic period silhouette. Use for texture/massing
  inspiration only (tile courses, bow-window rhythm), never as a direct likeness.
- _Licence:_ no licence concern (architecture photography of a public street), but the
  "no direct likeness" caveat above stands as a design constraint, not a legal one.

### D5 — "Billy la Banlieue" (confirmed by Bertrand as intended)

- [Wikipédia FR — Billy la Banlieue](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_la_Banlieue) —
  1986 Amstrad CPC platform game (Jean-Philippe Biscay, Loriciels): a loubard
  wandering the Paris métro hunting arcade cabinets; French cult classic of the same
  console era as _Prohibition_ itself.
- [billy-la-banlieue.com — fan/legacy site](https://www.billy-la-banlieue.com/) —
  secondary source for period screenshots and box art, same caveats as below.
- _Why it serves Belliard:_ a French sibling to _Prohibition_'s own console-era
  energy — period Paris banlieue/métro youth iconography (loubard silhouettes,
  RER-adjacent street life) as a graphic _mood_ touchstone.
- _Risk (named at Round 2, held per Bertrand's confirmation):_
  1. **Copyrighted commercial game** — any use stays at the level of period
     silhouette/mood, never traced character design or sprite likeness.
  2. **Decade bracket mismatch** — this is an 1986 reference, twelve years before our
     1998 setting; hold it against the opposite anachronism (too retro / not
     grimy-90s-street enough) rather than only checking for "too modern."
- _Licence:_ copyrighted commercial game (Loriciels/Infogrames-era French
  publishing) — **DO NOT DOWNLOAD OR TRACE** screenshots/sprites; describe and evoke
  mood only, same handling as `docs/art-direction/references/LICENSES.md` §1 treats
  _Prohibition_ itself.
